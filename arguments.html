arguments

Ten arguments about God, five for each side, each with the best reply. Not a weapon. A map of where the real fight is.

## before you argue

None of these is a proof. Each is a strong move that the other side has a serious answer to. You win a conversation by knowing the answer before it is given, and by conceding what is true.

Ask what would change their mind. Define the word “God” before you use it. State their best version, not their weakest. Remember that how someone came to believe a thing does not settle whether it is true.

For scale: in the 2020 PhilPapers survey of 1,770 philosophers, about two thirds accepted or leaned towards atheism. A head count is not an argument, but know it before you walk in. source

## for the believer, against the atheist

1. Fine-tuning

Life needs the strong force, gravity and the early expansion of the universe to sit inside very narrow ranges. A designer predicts that better than luck does.

Reply: there is no agreed way to measure the odds over all possible constants, so “improbable” is undefined. A multiverse, or the plain fact that only observers can observe, explains it without a designer.

SEP: Fine-Tuning

2. Contingency

Everything either explains itself or depends on something else. The universe does not explain itself. So something necessary stands behind it (Leibniz).

Reply: the universe may just be, a brute fact (Russell). Reasoning from the parts to the whole can be a fallacy of composition. And a necessary thing is not yet a God who cares.

SEP: Cosmological Argument · Russell–Copleston, 1948

3. Morality

If cruelty is wrong in every culture and every age, that needs a ground. A good God is a better ground than genes that only favoured survival.

Reply: the Euthyphro question. Is it good because God commands it, or does God command it because it is good? Secular moral realists such as Parfit also ground morality without God.

SEP: Moral Arguments · SEP: Theological Voluntarism

4. Consciousness

Physics describes structure and behaviour. It never says why any of it feels like something (Nagel, Chalmers). Mind first fits that better than mind last.

Reply: an unsolved problem is not evidence for God. Neuroscience keeps narrowing it, and non-theist answers exist. Even if mind is basic, that does not reach a God.

SEP: Consciousness

5. Trusting reason

If our minds were shaped only to survive, we would have no reason to trust them about deep truths, including naturalism itself (Plantinga).

Reply: reliable belief usually helps survival. Fitelson and Sober argued that Plantinga's probabilities are never actually supported.

Evolutionary argument against naturalism

## for the atheist, against the believer

1. Evil

Some suffering looks pointless, like an animal burning alone in a forest fire (Rowe). A God who is all-powerful and all-good would have stopped it.

Reply: free will and growth may need a world with real risk (Hick). Skeptical theism says we should not expect to see God's reasons. The rejoinder: that doubt weakens moral confidence too.

SEP: Problem of Evil · SEP: Skeptical Theism

2. Hiddenness

A loving God would make himself available to anyone open to him. Honest, open nonbelievers exist. So there is no such God (Schellenberg).

Reply: knowing that God exists is not the same as trusting him. Overwhelming evidence might force obedience, not love. And God may have reasons we cannot see.

SEP: Hiddenness of God

3. The shrinking gap

Lightning, plague, the variety of life: each was once credited to gods and each got a natural cause. Why would existence itself be different?

Reply: that is a pattern, not a proof. Science assumes laws and a universe to run them on, and why those exist is the question left. Both sides have to accept something unexplained.

SEP: Religion and Science

4. Geography of faith

Religions contradict each other, and most people hold the faith of the place they were born. That looks like upbringing, not discovery.

Reply: the genetic fallacy. Where a belief came from does not decide if it is true, and the same test applies to the atheist raised in a secular home.

SEP: Religious Diversity

5. Miracles

A report of a miracle must beat all the uniform experience against it. False reports are common. Broken laws of nature are not (Hume).

Reply: “uniform experience” assumes in advance that there are no exceptions. Critics such as Earman say the argument fails, and evidence has to be weighed case by case.

SEP: Miracles
